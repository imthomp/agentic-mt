**The central claim — does reasoning + tool use jointly beat either alone? — is still
completely untested.** Every direct attempt has hit a confound before producing a clean
result:

1. **Phase 3** (Aya-101, T5 seq2seq): CoT chain degenerates into verbatim self-repeats across
   draft/refine/proofread. A follow-up pretest (`scripts/phase3_pretest_format.py`) confirmed
   Aya-101 can't even be steered into producing structured `Analysis: / Translation:` output
   via few-shot prompting — 0/20.
2. **Phase 7** (BLOOMZ, decoder-only, en-fr + en-sw — `configs/pipelines_l1_conditions_bloomz_ensw.yaml`):
   ran to completion, but hit a **different** confound. CoT-only collapses to echoing the
   English source instead of translating. CoT+tool actively hallucinates unrelated content
   when the retrieved TM match is topically-adjacent-but-substantively-wrong (real example:
   a Vatican City population sentence pulled a Samoa-population TM match at cosine similarity
   0.50 — not a retrieval bug, just the ceiling of a small general-domain FLORES TM pool — and
   the model's multi-turn chain adopted the wrong match's translation as its own answer).
   Baseline beats every augmented condition on both pairs, same qualitative pattern as Phase 3
   but via a different mechanism. **Full numbers are in
   `results/pipelines_l1_conditions_bloomz_ensw/report.md` and the raw per-sentence
   prompts/outputs in `results/pipelines_l1_conditions_bloomz_ensw/runs/`, but this was never
   written up as `results/phase_7_summary.md` or folded into the advisor report — that's the
   single most concrete unfinished task.**

**GLM-5.2 cross-check: attempted, never completed.** Goal was to check whether Phase 6's
BLOOMZ exposure-floor finding (clean-generation rate is a hard threshold, not a gradient,
across en-fr/en-sw/en-yo/en-xh/en-zu — see `results/phase_6_summary.md`) is a small-model
artifact or holds at frontier scale. GLM-5.2 (753B MoE) is served locally via vLLM
(`/nobackup/autodelete/usr/it238/glm52/`, scripts there predate this session). Three separate
SLURM scheduling attempts over 24+ hours (m13h/qos=gpu, eng/qos=standby, piggybacking on a
peer Claude session's job via cross-session coordination) all failed — pending-job ETAs slid
backward instead of converging, and the one job we depended on got cancelled without
explanation, burning a full 24h client walltime polling a server that never existed. Gave up
on this path; **an 8-GPU-exclusive-node serving job appears to be a much harder scheduling
problem on this cluster than ordinary 1-4 GPU jobs** — don't assume it'll resolve within a
session.

As a zero-infrastructure stand-in, I (Claude, in-conversation, no tools) directly translated
the same 30 held-out sentences Phase 6 used (10 each for en-xh/en-zu/en-yo, seed=42) and did
not hit either of BLOOMZ's failure modes — no source-echoing, no repetition-loop collapse,
every output a genuine attempt at target-language content. This is suggestive that the
exposure floor is BLOOMZ-specific rather than inherent to these languages at any model scale,
but it is explicitly **not** a substitute for a real scored run: no independent QE, no
verified native-speaker accuracy check, self-generated and self-assessed.

## Honest framing, per the user's own repeated pushback

Most of the "findings" across six-plus phases are negative/expected results from small open
models (InkubaLM 0.4B, BLOOMZ 7B, Aya-101 13B) — "a 7B model trained on a curated corpus
doesn't know Zulu" is not surprising to anyone in the field on its own. The best candidate for
a genuinely new, non-obvious finding is the **cross-architecture CoT-chain fragility**: two
structurally different models (T5 encoder-decoder and BLOOM decoder-only) both broke on the
same decompose/draft/refine/proofread prompt design from Wu, Aycock & Monz (2025), via
different failure mechanisms. That pattern, if it holds up, says something about the prompt
design or about <15B open models generally — not really about the two-pillar theory itself,
which remains exactly as untested as when the project started.
